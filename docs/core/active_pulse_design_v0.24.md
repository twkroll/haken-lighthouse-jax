# CORE v0.24 — chart-safe active pulse design

## Scope

CORE v0.22 identified a severe spike-time weak direction in the six-dimensional inverse problem

\[
\vartheta=(p,\tau_3,\eta_1,\eta_2,\xi_\psi,\xi_q),
\]

with full-twelve-spike

\[
\sigma_{\min}(J_{\rm spike})\approx9.13748\times10^{-4}.
\]

CORE v0.23 showed that one direct transverse subthreshold readout can remove this bottleneck. v0.24 removes that readout again. The only observations are labelled spike times. Additional information is generated by one known, deterministic, pre-event input pulse.

The design problem is therefore active rather than passive.

## Pulse family

Let

\[
v_1=\frac1{\sqrt6}(2,-1,-1),\qquad
v_2=\frac1{\sqrt2}(0,1,-1),
\]

and define a unit zero-mean spatial direction

\[
d(\gamma)=\cos\gamma\,v_1+\sin\gamma\,v_2.
\]

At a prescribed pre-event time \(t_p\), the known pulse applies

\[
\psi^+=\psi^-+A\cos\beta\,d(\gamma),
\qquad
q^+=q^-+A\sin\beta\,d(\gamma).
\]

The pulse is not inferred. It is experimental control data.

The pulse norm is

\[
\sqrt{\|\Delta\psi\|^2+\|\Delta q\|^2}=A.
\]

The benchmark design box is

\[
0\le A\le0.1,\qquad t_p\ge0.05,
\]

with the hard hybrid-safety constraint

\[
m_{\rm chart}\ge0.06.
\]

The objective is E-optimal spike-time information:

\[
\max_{A,t_p,\beta,\gamma}\;\sigma_{\min}\bigl(J_{\rm spike}^{\rm pulse}\bigr).
\]

No derivative through event-order selection is used. For each candidate pulse, the physical packet/event scheduler records the admissible chart; fixed-chart one-sided derivatives are then evaluated inside that chart.

## Reference pulse

A refined constrained search places the optimum on the amplitude, earliest-time, and chart-margin boundaries. For robust reproducibility v0.24 stores a rounded interior design

\[
\boxed{t_p=0.05,\quad A=0.1,\quad\beta=0.55,\quad\gamma=-0.1.}
\]

Its spatial direction is approximately

\[
d(-0.1)\approx(0.81241750,-0.47680164,-0.33561586).
\]

The resulting state kick is

\[
\Delta\psi\approx(0.06926058,-0.04064851,-0.02861208),
\]

\[
\Delta q\approx(0.04246403,-0.02492181,-0.01754221).
\]

The physical truth-chart minimum margin is

\[
\boxed{m_{\rm chart}\approx0.06285675>0.06.}
\]

The first firing after the pulse is still far away,

\[
t_1\approx7.84779,
\]

so the pulse itself is not a near-event perturbation.

A refined boundary design near

\[
\beta\approx0.55720,\qquad\gamma\approx-0.09620
\]

reaches \(\sigma_{\min}\approx0.07957157\) at exactly the \(0.06\) margin constraint. The rounded reference loses only about \(1.8\times10^{-4}\) relatively in the objective.

## Information gain

For the rounded pulse, the full-twelve-spike sensitivity singular values are

\[
(10.30653,\,7.87048,\,2.70647,\,0.523591,\,0.265400,\,0.07955699).
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{\sigma_{\min}^{\rm pulse}=0.07955699}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\sigma_{\min}^{\rm pulse}}
{\sigma_{\min}^{\rm no\ pulse}}
\approx87.07.
}
\]

The condition number falls from about \(1.19\times10^4\) to about \(1.30\times10^2\).

This is weaker than the ideal direct readout of v0.23, which saturated the rank-one bound, but it requires spike times only.

At \(\sigma_t=10^{-4}\), the full-twelve-spike Fisher standard deviations become approximately

\[
(1.125,1.889,5.888,2.999,10.80,3.301)\times10^{-4}.
\]

Relative to the v0.22 no-pulse full-twelve experiment, the four latent-state marginal standard deviations improve by factors of approximately

\[
(102,100,79.7,25.4).
\]

## Hybrid-safety cost

A nearby pulse with \(\beta=0.55\), \(\gamma=-0.05\) yields

\[
\sigma_{\min}\approx0.07962170,
\]

but its chart margin is only

\[
m_{\rm chart}\approx0.03040368.
\]

Thus the unsafe design gains only about \(0.081\%\) in the E-optimal objective while sacrificing more than half the chart margin.

This establishes the v0.24 active-design rule:

> Hybrid safety is a hard design constraint, not a soft reward traded against an arbitrarily small information gain.

## Spike-only subset design after the pulse

The E-optimal six-spike schedule under the pulsed model is

\[
\boxed{(0,1,2,3,10,11)}.
\]

Its smallest singular value is

\[
\boxed{0.07483751},
\]

already close to the full-twelve value. The corresponding Fisher standard deviations at \(\sigma_t=10^{-4}\) are approximately

\[
(1.698,2.180,6.544,3.334,11.55,4.213)\times10^{-4}.
\]

## Weak-direction and nonlinear-alias rejection

The finite v0.22 weak-direction displacement of amplitude \(0.01\), previously nearly invisible to spike times, becomes distinguishable under the known pulse:

\[
\frac{\|\Delta Y_{\rm pulse}\|}{\sigma_t}\approx20.65.
\]

The exact same-chart nonlinear alias from v0.22 becomes

\[
\frac{\|\Delta Y_{\rm pulse}\|}{\sigma_t}\approx16.15.
\]

For the six-spike E-optimal schedule the corresponding norms are approximately \(15.13\sigma_t\) and \(11.88\sigma_t\).

The pulse therefore converts the previously hidden phase/synapse decomposition into observable spike-time differences without relying on a chart collision.

## Differentiation audit

The pulsed physical scheduler records the truth chart, and a JAX fixed-chart replay includes the deterministic pulse as an ordinary smooth state reset before the first physical event.

At the truth point:

\[
\max|Y_{\rm JAX}-Y_{\rm physical}|<10^{-12},
\]

and the six-parameter JAX Jacobian agrees with centered finite differences at relative error about

\[
\boxed{3.0\times10^{-9}}.
\]

The event-order/non-smoothness semantics remain unchanged from v0.19--v0.22.

## Nonlinear recovery and noise

A deterministic twelve-start local audit returns the true six-dimensional state/parameter vector for both the E-optimal six-spike pulse design and all twelve pulsed spikes.

At \(\sigma_t=10^{-4}\), thirty direct nonlinear fits agree with the Fisher scale for both schedules. For the six-spike design all marginal empirical/Fisher standard-deviation ratios are approximately between \(0.91\) and \(1.08\), except the \(\tau_3\) direction at about \(1.30\); for all twelve they remain approximately between \(0.83\) and \(1.23\).

## Interpretation

v0.23 solved the observability defect by changing the observation operator. v0.24 solves most of the same defect by changing the experiment while keeping the observation operator equal to spike time.

\[
\boxed{
\text{known pulse}
\to
\text{trajectory deformation}
\to
\text{transverse spike-time sensitivity}.
}
\]

The result is an active hybrid experiment-design benchmark, not a claim that every small pulse will improve identifiability.

## Scope and next step

v0.24 still assumes known pulse amplitude/time/direction, labelled spike times, known topology, and one real \(q=1\) latent synaptic direction in each of \(\psi_0,q_0\).

The next CORE step should test whether one or two chart-safe pulse experiments can identify the **full real two-dimensional q=1 synaptic subspace** in both \(\psi_0\) and \(q_0\), rather than immediately moving to unknown topology. The acceptance criterion should combine local rank/conditioning, nonlinear uniqueness, and chart-margin robustness across all experimental conditions.
