## 3. Spike-count lemma

**Label:** LEMMA / PROVED.  
**Provenance:** DERIVATION HERE FROM RB-004/RB-022.

For every neuron,

\[
0\le \dot\theta_i=S(\psi_i)<1.
\]

Consecutive lifted firing sections of one neuron differ by

\[
2\pi.
\]

If

\[
T_i^k<T_i^{k+1}
\]

are consecutive spikes of the same neuron, then

\[
2\pi
=
\theta_i(T_i^{k+1})-\theta_i(T_i^k)
=
\int_{T_i^k}^{T_i^{k+1}}S(\psi_i(t))dt
<
T_i^{k+1}-T_i^k.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
T_i^{k+1}-T_i^k>2\pi.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

### Corollary — finite spike count

**Label:** COROLLARY / PROVED.

The number \(K_i\) of spikes of neuron \(i\) strictly after \(t_0\) and not later than \(t_0+T_H\) obeys the conservative bound

\[
\boxed{
K_i
\le
1+\left\lfloor\frac{T_H}{2\pi}\right\rfloor.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
K_{\rm spike}
=
\sum_{i=1}^N K_i
\le
N
\left(
1+\left\lfloor\frac{T_H}{2\pi}\right\rfloor
\right).
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

No lower bound on \(S\) is required.

A neuron may simply stop firing when \(S=0\).

## 4. Arrival-count theorem

Let \(d_i^{\rm out}\) be the structural outdegree of neuron \(i\).

Every certified spike of neuron \(i\) launches exactly one labelled delayed arrival along every outgoing structural edge, including structural zero-weight edges.

Arrivals do not themselves launch new arrivals.

### Theorem — finite labelled arrivals

**Label:** THEOREM / PROVED.

The number of labelled arrivals that can occur in the finite horizon is bounded by

\[
\boxed{
K_{\rm arr}
\le
P_0
+
\sum_{i=1}^N d_i^{\rm out}K_i.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Consequently,

\[
K_{\rm arr}
\le
P_0
+
M
\left(
1+\left\lfloor\frac{T_H}{2\pi}\right\rfloor
\right)
\tag{4.2}
\]

is a coarser graph-wide bound.

### Proof

There are \(P_0\) initially pending arrivals.

Every later arrival must be generated by one of the finitely many horizon-relevant spikes and one of its finitely many outgoing structural edges.

Some generated arrivals may fall after the horizon, so counting all generated arrivals is an overbound. QED.

## 5. Finite-event theorem

**Label:** THEOREM / PROVED.

Under:

1. finite \(N,M\);
2. finite \(T_H\);
3. \(0\le S<1\);

5. finite certified horizon-relevant initial pending queue;

\[
\boxed{
K_{\rm event}
\le
K_{\rm spike}
+
K_{\rm arr}.

\tag{5.1}

Adding a horizon termination sentinel gives at most

\[
K_{\rm event}+1

   \[
   \overline B_i
   <
   \underline H.

### Proof

## 14. Certified next arrival rule

### NS-ARRIVAL

**Label:** THEOREM / PROVED AS A SUFFICIENT RULE.

Pending labelled arrival \(a_*\) is certified as the unique next ordinary event if:

   \[

   \underline H;

   \[
   \overline A_{a_*}.
   \tag{14.3}

The hypothetical propagation in (14.3) intentionally continues the pre-arrival flow to the latest arrival bound without applying the designated jump.

For any exact trajectory, the real pre-arrival flow agrees with that hypothetical flow until its actual arrival time.

Therefore (14.3) is sufficient to exclude every spike before \(a_*\).

### Proof

(14.1) excludes every other already launched arrival.

(14.3) excludes every spike before the designated arrival for every represented trajectory.

(14.2) excludes the horizon.

No other event class exists. QED.

# Part V — S5: constructive NNE-A

## 16. Segment-level NNE-A condition

### NNE-A-SEG

**Label:** DEFINITION / CONSTRUCTIVE CERTIFICATE CONDITION.

For one segment beginning at \(\mathcal C_m\) and ending at a designated next event \(E_{m+1}\), NNE-A-SEG holds when:

1. **Pending-arrival completeness**  
   \(\mathcal Q_m\) is certified to contain every previously launched structural arrival capable of occurring after the current event.

2. **Fixed-topology completeness**  
   every structural edge, including zero-weight edges, is represented in launch/arrival bookkeeping.

3. **Neuron completeness**  
   every neuron \(i=1,\ldots,N\) is explicitly covered by either:
   - the designated first-hit certificate, or
   - a no-hit certificate over the full prefix needed by NS-SPIKE/NS-ARRIVAL/NS-HORIZON.

4. **Strict designated-event ordering**  
   one and only one of NS-SPIKE, NS-ARRIVAL, NS-HORIZON is proved.

5. **No unlisted causal arrival before the designated event**  
   because a not-yet-pending arrival would require a source spike before the designated event, and every such non-designated spike is excluded by item 3.

6. **Post-event launch completeness**  
   if the designated event is a spike, every outgoing structural edge generates its labelled future arrival record.

7. **No zero-delay ordinary ambiguity**  
   if a newly launched arrival has zero delay, the spike/arrival simultaneity is classified as an ordinary collision rather than silently serialized.

## 17. NNE-A segment theorem

**Label:** THEOREM / PROVED.  
**Provenance:** DERIVATION HERE.

If NNE-A-SEG holds, there exists no unlisted ordinary physical event strictly between the current event and the designated next event.

### Proof

Assume for contradiction an unlisted event \(E'\) occurs earlier.

By the frozen event ontology, \(E'\) is either:

1. a spike; or
2. a structural arrival.

If it is a spike, it belongs to one of the explicitly enumerated neuron first-hit candidates and contradicts the designated/no-hit neuron coverage.

If it is an arrival, either:

- it was launched before the current event, in which case pending-arrival completeness requires it in \(\mathcal Q_m\), contradicting strict arrival ordering; or
- it is launched after the current event, which requires a source spike before \(E'\), hence before the designated event, contradicting neuron completeness.

No third event type exists. QED.

### Importance

This proves the missing no-new-event condition constructively.

It does not assume the desired tape is complete.

# Part VII — S7: whole-tape induction theorem

## 23. Completeness definition

A finite certified ordinary tape

\[
\mathcal T
=
(E_1,\ldots,E_K)
\tag{23.1}
\]

1. every physical spike strictly inside the certified horizon occurs exactly once in the tape with the correct neuron/section label;
2. every structural arrival strictly inside the horizon occurs exactly once with the correct source-spike/edge label;
3. no additional physical event lies between two consecutive tape events;
4. every spike is a certified first hit of its next lifted section and is transversal;
5. event labels are strictly ordered in the ordinary sequential sense;
6. all processed structural-zero arrivals remain represented;
7. the tape terminates through a certified horizon rule or at the last certified event before a separately certified event-free remainder to the horizon.

Completeness is a labelled-event statement.

It is stronger than reproducing only the continuous physical state.

## 24. Sequential completeness theorem

**Label:** THEOREM / PROVED.  
**Provenance:** DERIVATION HERE.

1. \(\mathcal C_0\) is a certified initial state/history with complete pending-arrival queue;

The post-event update encloses every exact post-event state and produces the complete pending queue for the next step.

Thus the induction hypotheses are restored at \(m+1\).

At the horizon rule, no remaining spike/arrival occurs before the horizon.

Therefore the complete physical event sequence is exactly the certified tape. QED.

# Part XII — S12: RIFC-TAPE-0.1

## 34. Algorithm name

\[
\boxed{
\text{RIFC-TAPE-0.1 — Sequential Certified Ordinary Event-Tape Builder}
}
\]

This is the single constructive algorithm specification frozen by this candidate theory.

It is not executed here.

## 35. Inputs

Required inputs:

1. fixed finite structural graph \(G=(V,E)\);
2. certified initial physical state enclosure;
3. current lifted section labels;
4. complete finite horizon-relevant pending-arrival queue;
5. fixed nonnegative delays or qualified delay intervals;
6. structural weight intervals, including zero crossings;
7. finite horizon;
8. exact singleton inputs or compact parameter-family box;
9. outward segment RIFC backend;
10. applicable arithmetic qualification identities/domains;
11. deterministic finite resource budgets for a production implementation.

## 36. Internal state

Maintain \(\mathcal C_m\) from Section 7 plus:

- accepted labelled tape prefix;
- event count;
- per-segment arithmetic qualification ID;
- refinement/precision level;
- NNE-A proof matrix;
- causal launch/arrival graph;
- certificate hashes/identities in a future implementation.

## 37. Deterministic candidate enumeration

At every step:

1. enumerate neurons in increasing index;
2. enumerate pending arrivals in a deterministic lexicographic causal-label order;
3. retain horizon as the final sentinel candidate;
4. never remove a structural-zero arrival because of its weight.

## 38. Deterministic segment certification cycle

At a refinement level:

### Step A — spike-safe prefix probes

Use the earliest rigorously possible pending-arrival/horizon boundary to define prefixes on which the physical state is certainly arrival-free.

Query RIFC for each neuron's first hit/no-hit status.

### Step B — attempt a unique next spike

For candidate spike labels in increasing neuron index:

- require NS-SPIKE;
- if exactly one candidate satisfies the proof, accept it.

No tie-breaking is performed between overlapping uncertified spike brackets.

### Step C — attempt a unique next arrival

For pending arrivals in deterministic causal-label order:

- extend the pre-arrival no-jump flow hypothetically through that arrival's latest bound;
- require no-hit for every neuron;
- require strict ordering against every other pending arrival and horizon;
- require NS-ARRIVAL.

If exactly one passes, accept it.

### Step D — attempt horizon termination

Require NS-HORIZON.

### Step E — otherwise refine/fail closed

If no unique candidate is certified:

- increase the prescribed refinement/precision level;
- recompute the necessary segment enclosures from the certified pre-event state.

If a finite resource limit is reached, return the relevant fail-closed class.

## 39. NNE-A proof record

For every accepted segment, retain:

1. complete pending-arrival inventory;
2. topology signature;
3. designated event label;
4. designated spike/arrival/horizon positive certificate;
5. one competitor-neuron no-hit record per required neuron;
6. all strict arrival-arrival/spike-arrival/horizon inequalities;
7. structural-zero arrival records;
8. proof that no future nonpending arrival can exist before the designated event without a forbidden earlier spike;
9. arithmetic qualification identity;
10. post-event queue-completeness record.

## 40. Post-event update

After accepting a spike:

- outward-propagate state to the hit bracket;
- preserve physical continuity;
- increment its section label;
- schedule one arrival on every structural outgoing edge;
- reject zero-delay simultaneity as ordinary collision.

After accepting an arrival:

- outward-propagate to the arrival;
- apply its alpha-state jump, including identity at zero weight;
- remove exactly that causal record.

After accepting horizon:

- terminate.

## 41. Stopping conditions

Positive completion:

\[
\texttt{CERTIFIED\_ORDINARY\_TAPE}
\]

only after a certified horizon termination and successful proof replay of every prior segment.

Nonpositive completion uses Section 32.

A partial tape is never relabelled as a complete tape.

A future positive tape artifact must include at least:

- model/topology identity;
- exact/interval initial data;
- horizon;
- initial pending history;
- ordered event labels;
- rigorous event-time intervals;
- spike first-hit and transversality certificates;

- proof ledger sufficient to replay each segment without using point estimates as authority.

## 45. Correlation-aware enclosure efficiency

**Label:** OPEN QUESTION.

Global event-time intervals may lose causal correlation.

The present theory does not mandate an enclosure representation beyond soundness.

## 46. Infinite prehistory

It does not derive that finiteness from arbitrary locally finite infinite history.

## 47. Finite-machine unconditional termination

**Label:** NON-RESULT.

No finite \(N_{\max}\), precision cap, memory cap, or runtime budget is proved sufficient for every strict ordinary trajectory.

## 48. Full chart radius

RB-024 indexed-margin plus NNE-A logic now has a constructive sequential realization, but this gate does not derive a sharp parameter-ball chart radius automatically.
